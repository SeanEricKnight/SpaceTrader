# Next human playtest

The automated simulations exercise rules and provide comparisons. They cannot tell us whether the game feels fair, readable, or worth reopening.

Recruit five to eight players, including both people familiar with Space Trader and newcomers. Record the game file’s SHA-256 digest, chosen difficulty, and device/browser so results can be matched to the actual build.

## Session 1: fifteen minutes without hints

Ask players to think aloud. Note when they recognize the moon goal, make their first profit, find price comparison, accept a contract, use the bank, and repair or refuel. Do not direct their route choice.

At five minutes, ask what they are trying to accomplish and what they intend to do next. At the first encounter, ask what made them fight, flee, or surrender.

## Session 2: thirty minutes of free play

Ask for an exported save at minutes 10 and 20 and at the end. Record credits, debt, trade profit, deliveries, ship, outfits, and visited ports. Note whether players keep repairing, plan contracts around a trade route, use financing, or fall into a repetitive ferry loop.

On Easy, watch whether ship damage feels recoverable and repairs are easy to find. A full-health simulation does not establish how well a player manages repeated encounters with a damaged ship.

## Questions afterward

1. What were you trying to accomplish?
2. When did you last feel clever?
3. Which route or cargo seemed best, and why?
4. What did you do about pirates? Did the warning help?
5. Did you buy or finance a ship or outfit? Did it help?
6. Did contracts support your trading plan or replace it?
7. Did the newspaper or planet histories change a decision?
8. When were you unsure what to do next?
9. What would you have done with another 1,000 credits?
10. Would you choose to open the game again tomorrow?

Report observations as **N of M players did X**. Keep guesses about motivation separate from what the player said or did. Rebalance only after reviewing the current build’s evidence, particularly when an older review describes already-replaced world generation.
