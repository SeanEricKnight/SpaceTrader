# Space Trader decisions

One entry per decision, newest first. Research method: shared-mind `memory/game-research-method.md` (find what loved games do, grade the evidence, then decide). Research log: shared-mind `memory/game-design-references.md`. Do not edit old entries to hide a reversal; add a new entry that supersedes it.

Entry format: **Date · Decision.** Decided by. Why (research link). Rejected. Status. How it is checked.

## 2026-11-16 · Hull warnings, a reason to go home, tiered events, one primary button per card
Decided by Sean ("approved" on each request), research by Claude. Why: low-health feedback research says show state distinctly and early, with the fix and its cost beside it (about 20 sources); hub and home-base design says a home must give something other than a discount, ideally recognition of what you did while away (about 30 sources: Hades, Sunless Sea, Pirates!, hub-and-spoke notes); button hierarchy guidance says one primary action per view, explain disabled states, group secondary actions (about 50 sources); FTL, Slay the Spire and RimWorld event design say vary the kind of decision, escalate with progress, never repeat back to back (about 40 sources). Chosen: amber/red hull states plus one sticky toast per level drop; Homecoming bonus of 75 cr per newly charted port and 10 free hull after 3+ days away; 24 events in tiers by jumps and difficulty with scaled stakes and falling odds; port card with one primary button. Rejected: a screen-red vignette (too loud for a trading game), a flat daily home income (rewards waiting, not travel), random-only event difficulty (RimWorld's own players complain it ignores context). Status: built in 0.25.0. How checked: `npm test` (all pass), headless Chrome at 390 px for the hull note, Homecoming payout (+225 cr, +10 hull for 3 new ports) and port card buttons, and a loop running every choice of every event at three difficulties and three jump counts (711 runs, no errors).

## 2026-11-16 · One world clock (per elapsed day), quotes from the same maths as the action, validate whole imports, Water does not count
Decided by Claude, delegated by Sean ("you decide after researching with github method"), after 8 sources and the existing sell-recovery decision (2026-11-15). Why: games that run a world economy tick it by elapsed time and catch up multiple ticks when more time passes (Sailwind updates prices daily as well as on trades; Offworld Trading Company applies base demand every fixed interval; No Man's Sky prices recover gradually after you sell; a game-dev write-up describes multi-tick updates for elapsed time), so a 2-day Quiet ride should move the world 2 days, as GAMEPLAY.md already said. Quotes: players distrust previews that differ from what happens (Eremite's trader-price tooltip request; brokerage "est." vs actual confusion), so the quote runs the exact selling formula. Imports: sources on safe import say a malformed import must not replace active state. Guide: sources on tutorials say state each step's goal clearly, so the step says "(not Water)" rather than silently ignoring Water. Contrary or weak: no source tests per-day vs per-jump recovery directly (design inference); one tutorial source argues for fewer forced steps (the guide is skippable). Rejected: per-jump clock (contradicts docs, punishes slow travel), counting Water as the first trade good (the tutorial already delivers Water, so it teaches nothing new), a full migration framework. Side effect accepted: Quiet rides slightly help dumping goods; they still cost two days of interest, rent and wages. Status: built in 0.24.9. Checked: `browser-preview/check-saves.cjs` (each fix reverted once).

## 2026-11-16 · Save integrity first: one effective-stats function, validate what the game can produce, repair rather than reject
Decided by Sean ("approved", after an independent ChatGPT code review and a 10-source check). Why: Meta's save-game guidance says version the schema, validate on load, keep backups and tell the player when one is used; a loader that rejects states the game itself creates is the failure all the corruption write-ups warn about (Bugnet series, one vendor, so counted once). RPG stat-system docs (Hytale entity stats, Bevy stats, Matter derived state) converge on one place that computes effective stats from base plus equipment, used by both gameplay and validation. Preview-must-match-result complaints are common in loved games (Last Epoch, Baldur's Gate 3, Gems of War, Klei's combat preview thread); bot-balancing write-ups (Slay the Spire 2 bot, CMU AI playtesting, Sims Mobile agents) warn absolute win rates from a simulator are unreliable unless the simulator's outcome definition is validated. Research improved the plan in two ways: (1) hard-reject only structural damage and trim out-of-range fuel/hull on load, so saves broken by the old removal bug heal instead of being thrown away; (2) a regression test that round-trips every generated state and fails if any fix is reverted. Rejected: removing validation, a save-format version bump (v1 still correct, the loader only got more accepting), changing the economy or combat balance from the old win rates, adding systems. Status: built in 0.24.8; quote/clock fixes deferred to BACKLOG. Checked: `browser-preview/check-saves.cjs` (each fix reverted once to prove the test catches it).

## 2026-11-16 · Every dimmed button explains itself on tap (central mechanism)
Decided by Sean ("yes" to the audit offer, after the event-card fix). Why: same 53 sources as the 0.24.5 entry below. A central click interceptor with a per-action reason table covers all 50+ disabled buttons without rewriting each one. Kept the real `disabled` attribute because the first-run guide, layout checks and CSS select on it; the cost is that dimmed buttons are not keyboard-focusable (event cards are). Correction to the entry below: GOV.UK says avoid disabled buttons; USWDS says weigh them carefully and follow best practice when you must use them. Rejected: converting all to aria-disabled (breaks `:not([disabled])` selectors, e.g. the guide glow), error-on-submit only. Status: built in 0.24.7. Checked: check-incidents (reason table) and a browser sweep of every tab (92 situations, 0 generic).

## 2026-11-16 · Accepting a job always says where to deliver, with a "Show me where" button
Decided by Sean ("I took a rush job and clicked a bubble to confirm, but it wasn't clear where to go to deliver"), after ~100 results. Why: Elite Dangerous players regularly ask "I accept a mission, then don't know where to travel"; the fix there is a mission marker plus Open Galaxy map. Starfield and The Outer Worlds offer an active-objective tracker and waypoint; Xbox Accessibility Guideline 109 asks for objective statements and markers. UX writing guides (Staffbase, Mintlify, ServiceTitan, Carbon) say confirmations should state what happens next and use a verb + noun button. Zeigarnik effect: open tasks stay in mind when progress is visible. Contrary: Dragon Age Veilguard players complain the tracker nags, and Obsidian forum players say markers replace good descriptions, so the message appears once at accept and stays until tapped, with no permanent arrows. Rejected: a permanent on-screen arrow. Status: shipped 0.24.6. Checked by check-layout.cjs (names destination, deadline and how to deliver; button opens the chart on it).

## 2026-11-16 · Message cards are 44px tappable with one optional action; travel lines are warm, dry and shuffled
Decided by Sean ("I leave it to you - approved") after ~55 sources. Why: WCAG 2.5.8 (24 px floor), Apple 44 pt and Material 48 dp targets; Humankind/Civ players want whole-card click and complain about tiny jump icons; IBM Carbon and Eleken say one optional action that opens the exact view; Hades and Failbetter on repetition and small text portions; Zoe Quinn and Zach Johnson on jokes dying on repeat; cozy design favours a consistent warm tone. Contrary: Civ/Stellaris notification overload, so actions are limited to events with a destination (only "New port logged" so far). Rejected: bottom placement (covers buttons, hard to reach top is acceptable because tapping is optional), funny jokes, logging every travel line (would flood the Activity log). Status: shipped 0.24.6. Checked by check-layout.cjs (card height, action then confirm, no back-to-back repeats, 20+ distinct lines).

## 2026-11-16 · Locked event options show the reason on the option and answer a tap
Decided by Sean's report ("I couldn't easily figure out that I needed fuel"; asked for research, then a fix), after 53 sources. Why: loved games (Fallout New Vegas, Disco Elysium, the Dialogue Tree docs) print the requirement inside the option; NN/g, GOV.UK and Primer say a disabled control must explain why and stay reachable; hover tooltips do not exist on phones. Details: shared-mind `memory/game-design-references.md`, entry "locked choices". Rejected: hiding locked options (players never learn them, FTL blue-option spreadsheets), hover tooltips (no hover on touch), error-on-submit only (suits forms, not a one-tap choice). Contrary evidence: GOV.UK and USWDS prefer not disabling at all; many sources are forum opinion. Status: built in 0.24.5 for event choice cards. Open: audit other disabled controls. Checked: check-incidents.

## 2026-11-16 · Event messages are sticky and stack at the top; routine confirmations time out
Decided by Sean ("they may need to stay until you click them ... a bubble showed up top and was gone before I could read it"), after ~58 sources. Why: players of GalCiv, Crate games, Dwarf Fortress and itch.io titles report vanishing popups as a bug and ask for a log; RimWorld letters stay until clicked and a Messages history keeps 200; IGDA accessibility guidance (configurable time, plus a log); WCAG 2.2.1 and Primer/GitHub on timed toasts; Lightning and ServiceTitan design systems keep toasts sticky except plain success; Material keeps snackbars short and low-priority. Contrary: Stellaris, CK3 and RimWorld players install mods to mass-dismiss notifications, so sticky is limited to event results, capped at four, with Dismiss all and Esc. Rejected: all toasts sticky; bottom placement (covered the buttons). Status: shipped 0.24.4. Checked by check-layout.cjs.

## 2026-11-16 · Locked ship cards lead with "Show me where", not a disabled Buy
Decided by Sean's question ("might need a button to go to the place this ship is available - don't just agree"), after ~60 sources. Why: NN/g and Baymard on dead ends (a disabled button with no next step; 30% leave when told only "out of stock"); Elite Dangerous players leave the game for Inara to find a shipyard and report an hour of wandering; X4 and Starfield put "buy ship" at the yard itself; implementation-intention meta-analysis (94 tests, d about 0.65) says naming where to act helps follow-through; Fogg: raise ability before motivation; card guidance: one primary action, at most two CTAs. Contrary: quest-marker critiques (Witcher 3, Cyberpunk) say too much guidance erodes exploration, so the button opens the map and does not travel or teleport; travel still costs fuel and days. Rejected: auto-travel, a second dead button pair, hiding Buy. Status: shipped 0.24.4. Checked by check-layout.cjs.
